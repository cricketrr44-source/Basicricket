Welcome to Basicricket

Cricket made easy for everyone

Cricket is played between two teams. The batting side tries to score runs, while the fielding side tries to limit runs and dismiss batters. Teams take turns batting and fielding. The team with the higher score wins, subject to the match format and result rules.

01

Two teams

A standard team has 11 players on the field, with permitted substitutes under the applicable rules.

02

One pitch

The pitch is 22 yards (20.12 metres) between the wickets.

03

One objective

Score more runs than the opposition and take wickets while following the Laws and playing conditions.

The essentials

Cricket rules

A plain-English overview of the 42 Laws of Cricket. Search a topic or filter by category.

Important: These are learning summaries, not the complete legal wording. International matches also use ICC playing conditions, which can vary by format and competition. Always check the current official rules for a specific match.

1. The players

A match is played by two teams. Each side normally has 11 players; team nomination and replacements follow the applicable rules.

2. The umpires

Umpires supervise the match, make decisions, manage time and ensure the Laws and playing conditions are followed.

3. The scorers

Scorers record runs, wickets, overs and other match details, checking signals and decisions with the umpires.

4. The ball

The match ball must meet the required size, weight and condition standards. The competition decides the approved ball type.

5. The bat

The bat must meet the permitted dimensions and construction requirements in the Laws and competition regulations.

6. The pitch

The pitch is a rectangular area 22 yards long and 10 feet wide, with wickets at each end.

7. The creases

Crease markings define the batters' safe ground and help umpires judge run-outs, stumpings and bowling positions.

8. The wickets

Each wicket consists of three stumps with two bails placed on top, subject to permitted alternatives in special circumstances.

9. Preparing the playing area

The match officials and ground staff oversee preparation and maintenance of the pitch and outfield.

10. Covering the pitch

Pitch covering is controlled by the Laws and the match's ground and weather arrangements.

11. Intervals

Intervals include breaks between innings and scheduled drinks or meal breaks, as set by the match arrangements.

12. Start and stop of play

Umpires control when play starts, pauses and ends, including for weather, light or other conditions.

13. Innings

An innings is a team's turn to bat. The number and length of innings depend on the match format.

14. The follow-on

In certain multi-innings matches, the team batting second may be required to bat again if it trails by the required margin.

15. Declaration and forfeiture

In formats that allow it, a captain may close their team's innings by declaration or forfeit it under the Laws.

16. The result

A match result may be a win, tie or draw, or may be abandoned or have no result, depending on the format and conditions.

17. The over

An over normally consists of six valid balls bowled from one end. No-balls and wides do not count as valid balls.

18. Scoring runs

Batters score by running between the wickets, hitting boundaries, or receiving extras. Completed runs and boundary allowances are added to the total.

19. Boundaries

A boundary is scored when the ball reaches or crosses the boundary under the Laws: normally four if it reaches the boundary after touching the ground, and six if hit over it without first touching the ground.

20. Dead ball

A dead ball is no longer in play. The umpire may call it dead in situations defined by the Laws.

21. No-ball

A no-ball is called for an illegal delivery, such as certain foot-faults or prohibited bowling actions. It gives the batting side an extra and may bring other penalties.

22. Wide ball

A wide is called when a delivery is too far from the striker to be hit with a normal cricket shot, judged under the relevant conditions. It gives the batting side an extra.

23. Bye and leg-bye

A bye is an extra when the ball passes the striker without touching the bat or body and batters run or it reaches the boundary. A leg-bye is scored after the ball contacts the striker's body, subject to the batter attempting to play or avoid the ball.

24. Fielder absence and substitutes

Substitutes may field in permitted circumstances, but do not automatically gain all the rights of a nominated player.

25. Batter's innings

A batter's innings begins when they come in to bat and ends when they are dismissed, retire under the Laws, or the innings ends.

26. Practice on the field

Practice is restricted during the match and must not waste time or damage the pitch or playing area.

27. The wicket-keeper

The wicket-keeper is the fielder positioned behind the striker's wicket and must follow rules about position and movement.

28. The fielder

Fielders may stop the ball and attempt dismissals, but must not field illegally or unfairly interfere with play.

29. The wicket is down

The wicket is broken when a bail is completely removed or a stump is struck out of the ground in the manner recognised by the Laws.

30. Batter out of their ground

A batter is out of their ground when no part of their body or bat is grounded behind the popping crease at that end.

31. Appeals

Fielders generally must appeal to the umpire before a batter can be given out, except where the Laws provide otherwise.

32. Bowled

The striker is out bowled if a legal delivery puts their wicket down, even if it first touches the bat or body in ways covered by the Law.

33. Caught

The striker is out caught if a fielder catches the ball fairly before it touches the ground, subject to the conditions in the Law.

34. Hit the ball twice

A batter must not deliberately strike the ball a second time, except to protect the wicket in the limited way allowed by the Law.

35. Hit wicket

The striker can be out hit wicket if they put their own wicket down in specified circumstances while receiving or setting off for a run.

36. Leg before wicket (LBW)

A batter may be out LBW if the ball hits their body rather than the bat and would have hit the stumps, subject to conditions about pitching, impact and shot offered.

37. Obstructing the field

A batter may be out if they wilfully obstruct or distract the fielding side, including by certain deliberate actions involving the ball.

38. Run out

A batter is run out if they are out of their ground and their wicket is fairly put down by the fielding side while the ball is in play.

39. Stumped

The striker may be stumped if they are out of their ground, are not attempting a run, and the wicket-keeper fairly puts the wicket down.

40. Timed out

An incoming batter must be ready within the time allowed by the applicable Laws and playing conditions, or may be given out timed out.

41. Unfair play

This Law covers unfair actions such as damaging the pitch, changing the condition of the ball unfairly, time-wasting and other prohibited conduct.

42. Players' conduct

Players must respect opponents, officials and the spirit of cricket. Misconduct can lead to warnings, penalties or disciplinary action.

Different ways to play

Three major formats

Multi-day cricket

Test cricket

Usually up to five days, with each team normally having two innings. A match can end in a win, tie or draw.

50 overs per side

One Day International (ODI)

Each team normally has one innings of up to 50 overs. The side scoring more runs wins, with special rules for interruptions and ties.

20 overs per side

Twenty20 (T20)

Each team normally has one innings of up to 20 overs. It is a shorter, fast-paced format with format-specific playing conditions.
